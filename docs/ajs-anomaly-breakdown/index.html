AJ’s Anomaly

Lead Designer/Developer – Unreal Engine 5

AJ’s Anomaly is a short, single player, spot-the-difference horror game for Steam, built in Unreal Engine 5. Developed with one other person, this game features interactive puzzles, jump scares, and narrative sequences that tell the story of a fashion designer overcoming her trauma through anomaly therapy.

RAPID PROTOTYPING AND FINDING THE FUN

I spent a short time ramping up in Unreal Blueprints before switching to prototyping gameplay exclusively in C++, without AI-tooling. Programming gameplay is a first for me but I took a course and was able to acclimate quickly and have been programming full-time since. I created about a dozen gameplay prototypes in first- and third-person, with systems like combat, AI-driven enemies, pickups, interactive maps, dialog, cinematics, data structures, and more before locking down the core feature set that we would move forward into production.

POLISHING AND FEEDBACK LOOPS

With the gameplay loop and content pipelines in place, we shifted some focus to polishing the UX/UI, character fidelity, and adding meta gameplay to facilitate a deeper narrative and build out a more complete player experience. Valuable feedback from early testers helped shape the pacing and narrative structure seen in the final version.

CO-DEVELOPING GAMES ON A TEAM OF 2

My colleague and I divided development roles and responsibilities nearly evenly. I am responsible for gameplay programming, game design, and UI, and he is responsible for characters, sound, and narrative elements. We worked from a shared Git repo, tasked ourselves in Trello for visibility, and met on a regular basis to discuss priorities, discoveries, and more. We built this game remotely in about 3 months.
